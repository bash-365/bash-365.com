When your software belongs to a vendor, every release is their operations decision landing on your desk, and the people closest to the work get the least say.

The planner loses the shortcut. IT absorbs the blame for a release it never scheduled. Management sees the real shape of it: once the ledger, work orders and customer history live in one rented system, leaving costs a second implementation.

So the old rule, don't build what you can buy, deserves a fresh look. It held because building was expensive. AI coding agents have cut much of that cost. What they have not cut is judgment: someone still has to know which process is load-bearing and which is habit.

A practical test for your next renewal or migration: buy what is the vendor's core craft, such as payroll or tax tables. Be careful with anything that is your own craft wearing a vendor's label, like the costing method your controller defends to an auditor.

The essay walks through what year one and year five look like on each path.

Worth a read before you sign the next renewal.
