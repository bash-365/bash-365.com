Most small-business AI projects don't fail on the model. They fail on three things around it: data nobody trusts, a workflow nobody owns, and a cost nobody budgeted.

Data comes first. Many small firms hold the same revenue number in three places, the accounting system, the customer relationship management (CRM) system and the controller's spreadsheet, and the three disagree. Cleaning that up routinely takes more than half of a project's effort. A model trained on it gives confident wrong answers, which is worse than no model at all.

Change comes second. A pilot that produces a useful weekly report is not a project until someone's job changes to use it. We see pilots prove their value, collect the applause, and quietly die because month-end close still runs the old way.

Cost comes third. The license fee is the line everyone plans for. Running the model, monitoring it, and paying someone to review its output each quarter are the lines that show up after launch.

The takeaway you can use this week: before any pilot, pick one process and one number with a baseline, then decide which system is the source of truth for that number. It is unglamorous plumbing, and it pays off even if you never buy the AI.

The full piece covers a staged approach that fits a 10 to 200 person business, where AI is paying off in services and distribution work, and the watch-outs to check before you sign anything.
