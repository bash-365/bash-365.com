# Research — what the evidence says about answering reviews

The research stage of [Every review answered](README.md), compiled 2026-11-26. Every figure the campaign uses comes from here, with its source. Every finding below comes from a page or PDF that was actually opened ("opened: yes"); where a site refused automated access and the page was read through an Internet Archive snapshot, that is said. Vendor-run research is labelled **vendor-run**: BrightLocal sells local search and review software, so its surveys are vendor-run too, even though they are the industry's standard reference. Quotes are kept under 30 words. The sections "Could not verify" and "Best five facts for a restaurant owner" at the end say what not to use and what to lead with.

## 2. Revenue impact of ratings for restaurants

**2.1 Luca (HBS working paper 12-016).** For independent restaurants, a one-star Yelp increase causes a 5–9% revenue increase. Chains show no effect.
- Abstract: "(1) a one-star increase in Yelp rating leads to a 5-9 percent increase in revenue, (2) this effect is driven by independent restaurants; ratings do not affect restaurants with chain affiliation"
- Conditions: the data is "revenues for all restaurants in Seattle from 2003 through 2009" from the Washington State Department of Revenue. The identification is a regression discontinuity around Yelp's half-star rounding: "Yelp presents the average rating for each restaurant, rounded to the nearest half-star."
- Size by specification: "a one-star increase leads to a 5-9 percent increase in revenue for independent restaurants, depending on the specification." In the main regression-discontinuity estimate, "an exogenous one-star improvement leads to a roughly 9% increase in revenue. (Note that the shock is one-half star, but I renormalize…)"
- Chains: "the impact is statistically insignificant and close to zero for chains."
- Source: Michael Luca, *Reviews, Reputation, and Revenue: The Case of Yelp.com*, Harvard Business School Working Paper 12-016, © 2011, 2016. PDF: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/12-016_a7e4a5a2-03f9-490d-b093-8f951238dba2.pdf (opened: yes). Record: https://ideas.repec.org/p/hbs/wpaper/12-016.html (opened: yes; lists "2011, revised March 2016").
- **Campaign caution:** say "independent restaurants" and "a full star." The causal shock is a half-star at a rounding threshold, rescaled to one star. The data is Seattle and Yelp, 2003–2009, not Google today.

**2.2 Anderson & Magruder (Economic Journal, 2012).** On Yelp, an extra half-star makes restaurants sell out of reservations much more often.
- Abstract: "An extra half‐star rating causes restaurants to sell out 19 percentage points (49%) more frequently, with larger impacts when alternate information is more scarce."
- The outcome is restaurant **reservation availability** (sell-outs), not revenue. The design is a regression discontinuity on Yelp's rounded ratings.
- Source: Michael Anderson and Jeremy Magruder, "Learning from the Crowd: Regression Discontinuity Estimates of the Effects of an Online Review Database," *The Economic Journal* 122(563), September 2012, pp. 957–989 (published online Mar 9, 2012). https://academic.oup.com/ej/article/122/563/957-989/5079476 (opened: yes; DOI 10.1111/j.1468-0297.2012.02512.x)
- **Campaign caution:** "19 percentage points" is not "19%". The relative increase is 49%.

## 3. Effect of responding to reviews

**3.1 Proserpio & Zervas (Marketing Science, 2017). Hotels, not restaurants.** Hotels that start responding see slightly higher ratings and more reviews.
- Abstract: "we find a 0.12-star increase in ratings and a 12% increase in review volume for responding hotels."
- Also: "when hotels start responding, they receive fewer but longer negative reviews" and "hotels respond to positive, negative, and neutral reviews at roughly the same rate."
- Source: Davide Proserpio and Georgios Zervas, "Online Reputation Management: Estimating the Impact of Management Responses on Consumer Reviews," *Marketing Science* 36(5), Sept 2017, pp. 645–665. Abstract record: https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v36y2017i5p645-665.html (opened: yes). The publisher page https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2017.1043 returned 403.

**3.2 Chevalier, Dover & Mayzlin (Marketing Science, 2018). Hotels, not restaurants.** Responding stimulates more reviewing, especially more negative reviews. This is a counterweight to 3.1.
- Abstract: "we show that reviewing activity and particularly negative reviewing is indeed stimulated by managerial response."
- Mechanism: "managers respond more and in more detail to negative reviews."
- Design: "comparison of the same hotel immediately before and after response initiation."
- Source: Judith A. Chevalier, Yaniv Dover and Dina Mayzlin, "Channels of Impact: User Reviews When Quality Is Dynamic and Managers Respond," *Marketing Science* 37(5), Sept 2018, pp. 688–709. https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v37y2018i5p688-709.html (opened: yes). Working-paper version, NBER w23299 (March 2017): https://www.nber.org/papers/w23299 (opened: yes)
- **Honest read of 3.1 and 3.2:** the hotel evidence says replies change who reviews and how. The rating gain is small (0.12 stars) and comes with more, and longer, negative feedback.

**3.3 Kumar, Qiu & Kumar (Information Systems Research, 2018). Includes restaurants.** Only businesses that actually use the owner-response feature gain check-ins, and there are spillovers onto nearby competitors.
- Abstract: "Only the businesses that choose to use the management response feature observe increases in check-ins."
- Also: "the businesses that are unaware of the management response feature launch… or are aware but choose not to use the management response feature, tend to remain at a disadvantage."
- Restaurant context (university press release): "in analyzing the performance of nearby restaurants in direct competition, businesses that directly engaged with customers online increased their number of check-ins"
- Sources: Naveen Kumar, Liangfei Qiu and Subodha Kumar, "Exit, Voice, and Response on Digital Platforms: An Empirical Investigation of Online Management Response Strategies," *Information Systems Research* 29(4), Dec 2018, pp. 849–870. https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orisre/v29y2018i4p849-870.html (opened: yes). Temple University Fox School news, Oct 16, 2017: https://www.fox.temple.edu/news/2017/10/online-management-responsiveness-good-business (opened: yes)
- No effect size is quoted, because the full paper is paywalled and was not opened.

**3.4 Cao, He & Jin (NBER Working Paper 34934, 2026). Restaurants.** Restaurants that adopted an automated negative-review alert system improved their ratings. The adoption partly replaced public replies.
- Abstract: "adoption increases average star ratings, lowers the share of negative reviews, and raises the positivity of review text."
- On replies: "ARMS adoption partially crowds out front-end managerial responses, indicating a substitution between operational remediation and public replies"
- Source: Guangyu Cao, Shenghao He and Ginger Zhe Jin, "From Complaint to Action: Technology-Enabled Quality Improvement from Consumer Reviews," NBER WP 34934, March 2026 (revised October 2026). https://www.nber.org/papers/w34934 (opened: yes)
- **Caveats:** the system studied is an alerting and work-ticket tool from an anonymous company, not a reply-drafting tool. The abstract does not state the country or the platform.

**3.5 Blind test of AI versus human replies, using a restaurant review** (vendor-run). Most consumers preferred the ChatGPT-written reply to a real owner's reply.
- "58% of consumers unknowingly chose the AI-generated review response as their preferred response!" The test used a positive review of "a Mexican restaurant." Sample: "1,141 US consumers via SurveyMonkey."
- Source: *Local Consumer Review Survey 2024*, BrightLocal, 2024. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2024/ (opened: yes)
- The 2025 edition repeated the test with a vet clinic and found results "almost identical year on year." Source: *Local Consumer Review Survey 2025*, BrightLocal (sample 1,026). https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/ (opened: yes)

## 4. Google's own words

### 4(a) Business Profile Help on reading and replying to reviews

- **4a.1** Google frames replies as a signal of responsiveness: "Helpful and positive replies to reviews can show that you're responsive to your customers." Replies are screened against policy: "If your reply isn't approved to be posted, you'll be asked to edit it. Replies usually take up to 10 minutes to review, but sometimes a review can take up to 30 days." Reviewers are notified: "The person who wrote the review is notified when you reply." Replying requires a verified business: "Before you can reply to reviews, you must verify your business." The page does **not** mention AI.
  - Source: "Manage customer reviews," Google Business Profile Help, Google, 2026. https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en (opened: yes)
- **4a.2** Google's reply-writing tips discourage generic thank-yous: "Instead of sending the same 'thank you' to everyone, focus on reviews where you can share a helpful update or answer a question." Other tips:
  - Keep it short: "If a reply is too long, people might stop reading before they get to the main point."
  - No promotions: "avoid using your response to offer deals or promotions."
  - Privacy: "Never share a reviewer's private information or engage in personal attacks."
  - Personalize: "Address the reviewer by name and acknowledge their specific feedback."
  - Timeliness: "A prompt response shows that you value your customers' feedback and are committed to high-quality service."
  - The page also says: "As best practice, reply to reviews and share your perspective." It does not mention AI.
  - Source: "Tips to get more reviews" (section "Tips to write better review replies"), Google Business Profile Help, Google, 2026. https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en (opened: yes)

### 4(b) "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google"

- **4b.1** The page says: "Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity." The live page says "popularity" in that sentence but keeps "Prominence" as the section heading.
- **4b.2** The three factors:
  - Relevance: "Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for."
  - Distance: "Distance refers to how far each business is from the customer who's searching."
  - Prominence: "Prominence means how well-known a business is."
- **4b.3** Reviews feed prominence and ranking: "This factor's also based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have. More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking."
- **4b.4** Responding is listed as a ranking tip: "When you reply to customer reviews, it shows that you value their feedback. Positive reviews and helpful replies can help your business stand out." Google does **not** say that replies directly raise rank.
- **4b.5** "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."
- Source: "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google," Google Business Profile Help, Google, 2026. https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en (opened: yes)

### 4(c) Prohibited review practices (Maps user-generated content policy)

- **4c.1 Paying or incentivizing for reviews is banned.** Merchants may not "Offer incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services - in exchange for posting any review or revision or removal of a negative review." Banned content includes "Reviews or ratings that have been paid for, directly or in kind."
- **4c.2 Review gating and selective solicitation are banned.** Merchants may not "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers"
- **4c.3 Pressure and staff quotas are banned.** "merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises, nor should they request that specific content be included." This includes "Merchants requesting that staff solicit a certain number of reviews."
- **4c.4 Conflict-of-interest and insider reviews are banned.** "A conflict of interest may include current or former employment, a contractual or consultory relationship, or other professional or personal affiliations"
- **4c.5 What is allowed.** "Solicit or encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating"
- Source for 4c.1–4c.5: "Prohibited & restricted content," Maps User Generated Content Policy Help, Google, 2026. https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en (opened: yes)
- **4c.6 Penalties for fake engagement.** Examples: "Business Profile will not be able to receive new reviews or ratings for set period of time" and "Business Profile will display a warning to let consumers know that fake reviews were removed"
  - Source: "Business Profile restrictions for policy violations," Google Business Profile Help, Google, 2026. https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287?hl=en (opened: yes)
- **4c.7** Google's own help also says incentives to "post reviews, change reviews, or remove negative reviews" are "strictly prohibited." Source: https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en (opened: yes)

Under "Review replies" it reads: "Draft or delete replies to customer feedback." The example prompt is "Help me draft a reply to my latest review." Limits:

  - Source: "Save time and grow your business with new Gemini tools," Vishnu Sivaji, The Keyword (blog.google), Google, Jun 10, 2026. https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-features-for-businesses/ (opened: yes)

The option's text: "Use Google AI to create personalised replies that build trust." A tester said the option "wasn't available" for other unanswered reviews.
  - Source: Barry Schwartz, "Google Tests Reply To Reviews With AI," Search Engine Roundtable, Mar 24, 2026. https://www.seroundtable.com/google-reply-to-reviews-with-al-41095.html (opened: yes)
- **4d.4 Absence check.** As of 2026-11-26, Google's "Manage customer reviews" and "Tips to write better review replies" help pages do not mention AI replies (see 4a; text searched for "AI," "Gemini," and "suggest"). The "What's new in Google Business Profile" index stops at April 2025: https://support.google.com/business/topic/15283988?hl=en (opened: yes)
- **Bottom line for positioning:** BASH cannot claim that "Google doesn't do this." Google now drafts replies for free in Gemini for single-profile owners, and is testing a dashboard button. The honest gaps:
  - Gemini is unavailable to anyone managing more than one verified profile, to work or school accounts, and in the EEA and UK.
  - Google documents no approval workflow, audit trail, or multi-person sign-off.
  - Gemini can also delete replies.

## 5. Law

### FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465)

- **5.1 Effective date.** "This rule is effective October 21, 2024." It was published in the Federal Register on 2024-08-22 at 89 FR 68034.
  - Source: Federal Register API record for document 2024-18519, "Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials," FTC, 2024. https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2024-18519.json (opened: yes). Human page: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials (redirected to a bot check, not opened)
- **5.2 Fake reviews (§ 465.2).** A business may not "write, create, or sell a consumer review… that materially misrepresents… That the reviewer or testimonialist exists" or that they had experience with the business. The FTC press release says this covers "such as AI-generated fake reviews."
- **5.3 Insider reviews.** § 465.2(c) bars procuring reviews from "officers, managers, employees, or agents, or any of their immediate relatives" that the business knew or should have known misrepresent the reviewer's existence or experience. § 465.5 requires "clear and conspicuous disclosure of the officer's or manager's material relationship to the business."
- **5.4 Buying reviews (§ 465.4).** A business may not "provide compensation or other incentives in exchange for, or conditioned expressly or by implication on, the writing or creation of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment"
- **5.5 Review suppression (§ 465.7).** Barred: "an unfounded or groundless legal threat, a physical threat, intimidation, or a public false accusation" used to prevent or remove a review. Also barred: misrepresenting that displayed reviews are all reviews "when reviews are being suppressed… based upon their ratings or their negative sentiment."
  - Source for 5.2–5.5: 16 CFR Part 465, eCFR, current as retrieved 2026-11-26 (Source: 89 FR 68077, Aug. 22, 2024). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465 (opened: yes)
- **5.6 Civil penalties.** The rule lets the FTC "seek civil penalties against knowing violators." Effective timing: "60 days after the date it's published in the Federal Register."
  - Source: "Federal Trade Commission Announces Final Rule Banning Fake Reviews and Testimonials," FTC press release, Aug 14, 2024. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials (opened: yes)
- **5.7 Review gating is not banned by the rule itself, but the FTC warns it may still be illegal.** Asked "Can my business ask for reviews only from customers whom we think are happy with our services?", the FTC answers: "The rule does not contain a specific prohibition against such conduct. But this practice could violate the FTC Act." (Google's policy bans it outright; see 4c.2.)
- **5.8 Replying is allowed, carefully.** Asked about responding to a negative review: "Yes, you can respond publicly to the review, and yes, you should watch what you say." The FTC page states the rule "went into effect on October 21, 2024."
  - Source for 5.7–5.8: "The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers," FTC Business Guidance, November 2024. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers (opened: yes)

### HIPAA and replying to reviews (healthcare clients)

- **5.9 Elite Dental Associates (Dallas), 2019: $10,000.** A dental practice paid $10,000 after disclosing patient information in Yelp review replies. OCR "found that Elite had impermissibly disclosed the protected health information (PHI) of multiple patients in response to patient reviews on the Elite Yelp review page." The original complaint alleged a reply disclosed "the patient's last name and details of the patient's health condition." The settlement also requires "two years of monitoring by OCR." OCR Director Roger Severino said: "Doctors and dentists must think carefully about patient privacy before responding to online reviews."
  - Source: "Dental Practice Pays $10,000 to Settle Social Media Disclosures of Patients' Protected Health Information," HHS press release, Oct 2, 2019. https://www.hhs.gov/about/news/2019/10/02/dental-practice-pays-10000-settle-social-media-disclosures-of-patients-phi.html (opened: yes, via the Internet Archive snapshot of 2020-12-21, because hhs.gov returned "Access Denied" to automated requests). Agreement page: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/elite/index.html (opened: yes, via Internet Archive snapshot of 2026-07-11)
- **5.10 Manasa Health Center (New Jersey), 2023: $30,000.** A psychiatric practice paid $30,000 over PHI disclosed in a reply to a negative review. The complaint alleged the practice "impermissibly disclosed the protected health information of a patient when the entity posted a response to the patient's negative online review." "Manasa Health Center paid $30,000 to OCR and agreed to implement a corrective action plan." The page was last reviewed June 5, 2023.
  - Source: "HHS Office for Civil Rights Reaches Agreement with Health Care Provider in New Jersey That Disclosed Patient Information in Response to Negative Online Reviews," HHS, 2023. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/manasa/index.html (opened: yes, via Internet Archive snapshot of 2026-10-25)

## 6. Restaurant industry context

- **6.1 Size.** The NRA describes an industry of "more than 1 million restaurant and foodservice outlets and a workforce of 15.7 million employees." Its 2026 forecast: "total restaurant and foodservice sales are projected to reach $1.55 trillion."
- **6.2 Margins are thin.** "Last year, 42 percent of operators reported their restaurant was not profitable"
  - Source for 6.1–6.2: "Persistent Cost Increases and Enduring Demand Will Shape the Restaurant Industry in 2026," National Restaurant Association press release, Feb 12, 2026. https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/media/press-releases/persistent-cost-increases-and-enduring-demand-will-shape-the-restaurant-industry-in-2026/ (opened: yes)
- **6.3 Most restaurants are single-unit, small businesses.** "7 in 10 restaurants are single-unit operations" and "9 in 10 restaurants have fewer than 50 employees." The page bases its economic figures on "publicly available 2022 data." "Single-unit" is the NRA's term and is not identical to "independent."
  - Source: "National Statistics," National Restaurant Association, accessed 2026-11-26. https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/industry-statistics/national-statistics/ (opened: yes)
- **6.4 Owner response rates (self-reported, vendor-run).** About half of small businesses say they respond to reviews. Food and drink businesses are slightly ahead.
  - "only 51% of business owners actually respond to reviews." In the industry table, the "Respond to reviews" row reads **Food or drink: 60%** (all businesses 51%; automotive highest at 63%).
  - Also: "14% said they don't really do anything, while 6% didn't even know they could manage or respond to Google reviews."
  - Food or drink businesses: 35% have fewer than 20 Google reviews, against 47% of all businesses.
  - Methodology: "a representative panel of 1,002 US-based owners of locally operating businesses with 1-50 employees."
  - Source: "Small businesses can't keep up with consumer review expectations" (Small Business Marketing Report 2026, ch. 2), BrightLocal, Oct 21, 2026. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/smb-report-enough-reviews/ (opened: yes)
- **6.5 Restaurant owner-reply rate on Google (vendor-run, customer data).** About one restaurant review in four gets a reply. Restaurants reply to 26% of Google reviews: "Three of every four restaurant reviews get no owner response." The data covers "816,307 Google reviews… across 3,739 US local businesses," about 430 of them restaurants, all customers of the vendor. That is not a random sample.
  - Source: "Google Reviews by Industry: What 816,000 Reviews Reveal (2026)," ProsperQR, Jul 14, 2026 (updated Jul 24, 2026). https://prosperqr.com/blog/google-reviews-by-industry (opened: yes)
- **6.6 Restaurant reply rates are rising (vendor-run, regional).** "restaurants from 9% to 42%" of reviews answered between 2019 and 2026. Sample: "a random sample of 400 businesses in Greater Boston and MetroWest," with 100 restaurants.
  - Source: "Local Businesses Now Answer Twice as Many Google Reviews, Revfer Study Finds," press release syndicated by Middletown Life Magazine, Nov 4, 2026. https://lifestyle.middletownlifemagazine.com/story/807689/local-businesses-now-answer-twice-as-many-google-reviews-revfer-study-finds/ (opened: yes)
- **6.7 Time pressure (all small businesses, vendor-run).** "Nearly a quarter of business owners (23%) said they don't have enough time to improve their online presence."
  - Source: Small Business Marketing Report 2026, ch. 4, BrightLocal, 2026. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/smb-report-who-owns-marketing/ (opened: yes)
- **6.8 Hours per week spent on reviews: no credible figure found.** See "Could not verify."

**Positioning notes drawn from the above (analysis, not a sourced claim):**
- "AI drafts, a person approves" is not unique. Toast has exactly that for restaurants on Toast, Birdeye and Yext offer it as a configuration, and Google's Gemini drafts for single-profile owners.
- What the sources do support as differentiators:
  - **Approval is mandatory, not optional.** Birdeye's product page auto-publishes 4- and 5-star replies, and Yext can auto-send.
  - **No point-of-sale lock-in.** Toast requires Toast IQ Grow per location.
  - **Multi-location and team accounts.** Gemini excludes these.
- Google's API policy (4e.4) requires "prior specific and express consent" for automated replies, which a human-approval gate satisfies by design.
- Google's 30-day storage limit (4e.5) constrains how long synced reviews may be kept.

## Could not verify

- **Hours per week that restaurant owners or managers spend on reviews.** Every figure found came from vendor blogs (for example "10-15 hours," "3-4 hours," "2–6 hours/week") with no published methodology. A "4.3 hours per week" figure attributed to BrightLocal could not be found in any BrightLocal publication. **Do not use any of these.**
- **Census restaurant establishment count** (NAICS 7225). The Census API now requires a key, so no Census figure was retrieved. Use the NRA's "more than 1 million restaurant and foodservice outlets" instead. Note that it counts all foodservice outlets, not only restaurants.
- **A restaurant-specific share of consumers who read reviews, newer than 2020.** BrightLocal's industry table was last published in 2020. The NRA 2026 figure covers summer travelers only.
- **Search Engine Land's report on Google's AI-reply test** (https://searchengineland.com/google-business-profile-test-reply-to-reviews-with-ai-472167). The page returned 403 and a Cloudflare challenge. Secondary claims that the test appeared in "the U.S., Brazil, and India" and that replies could be triggered "in bulk" are unverified.
- **Whether Google's Gemini review-reply drafts require explicit confirmation before posting.** Neither Google page says.
- **Toast IQ Grow's current price.** Toast's own product, news and BusinessWire pages blocked automated access. $499/month comes only from trade press (Pulse 2.0). A blog claim that the price rose to $699/month in October 2026 was not opened or verified.
- **Yext list prices.** The official pricing page is a 404. Aggregator figures ($199–$999/yr) are unverified.
- **Podium and Reputation approval workflows.** Their pages say AI drafts replies but do not say whether a human must approve.
- **Owner.com review-reply features.** None found. Absence on the pricing page is not proof the feature doesn't exist.
- **FTC civil-penalty dollar amount per violation.** It is commonly cited (figures around $50,000+), but no FTC page stating the current inflation-adjusted amount was opened.
- **New Vision Dental HIPAA settlement** (often cited alongside Elite Dental). The archived HHS page failed to load, so it is not used.
- **Proserpio & Zervas's TripAdvisor-versus-Expedia design detail** and the full papers behind Kumar et al. (2018) and Chevalier et al. (2018). The publisher sites are paywalled or blocked, so only abstracts were used. No effect size is cited for Kumar et al.
- **Commonly misquoted versions of verified studies.** Do **not** use these:
  - "Harvard found a one-star increase raises revenue 5–9% for any business." Luca's result is for independent restaurants in Seattle on Yelp, 2003–2009, and chains showed no effect.
  - "Half a star makes restaurants 19% more likely to sell out." The finding is 19 percentage points, a 49% relative increase.
- **Any study showing that responding to reviews raises restaurant revenue.** None was found. The response studies show effects on ratings, review volume and composition (hotels), check-ins (Kumar et al.), and review sentiment (Cao et al.). None measures revenue.

## Best five facts for a restaurant owner

1. **Customers expect a reply, and fast.** 89% of consumers expect business owners to respond to reviews. 32% want a response by the next day, up from 18% in 2025, and 81% within a week. *BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (vendor-run), 1,002 US adults, Feb 2026.*
2. **Four stars is now the floor for most diners' shortlists.** 68% will only use a business with four or more stars, up from 55% in 2025, and 31% require 4.5 or more. *BrightLocal LCRS 2026.*
3. **Stars move revenue for independents.** A one-star Yelp increase leads to a 5–9% revenue increase for independent restaurants; chains showed no effect. *Luca, HBS Working Paper 12-016 (Seattle, 2003–2009).*
4. **Canned replies backfire, so drafts need a human.** Templated or generic responses make 50% of consumers unlikely to choose a business. Google's own guidance says not to send "the same 'thank you' to everyone." *BrightLocal LCRS 2026; Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to write better review replies."*
5. **Google ties reviews to local visibility.** "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking," and "Positive reviews and helpful replies can help your business stand out." *Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google."*

Runner-up: an extra half-star on Yelp causes restaurants to sell out 19 percentage points (49%) more often. *Anderson & Magruder, Economic Journal, 2012.*
