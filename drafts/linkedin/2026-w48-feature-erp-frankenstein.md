Your warehouse can stop a plant, and the code that runs it may be editable by one contractor, awake at the wrong hour. That is the part of the ERP sprawl nobody puts on a keynote slide.

Picture one user shipping one pallet. A web screen for order entry, a thick client for financials, two more for assets and scheduling, and a green-screen warehouse module for labels, scanning and receiving. Six interfaces from four decades, and the fix for the oldest one lives in a niche language (Progress 4GL) that few people still read.

The cost shows up quietly. Contractors keep the oldest pieces alive, an internal IT group spends its days on upkeep rather than the modernization the business asked for, and the people who understand the old code are retiring. A receiving failure at 2 PM Colorado time lands at 1:30 AM for the developer who can fix it, and by morning you have lost a shift.

The takeaway is to stop treating the sprawl as one mass. Spend two to four weeks mapping every interface, custom routine and integration. Rank each by business criticality, talent risk and contract cost. Then decide piece by piece what to replace, wrap or leave alone, and move the warehouse last, with a tested fallback and a parallel run.

The full piece covers the three places a "just move to the cloud" plan usually dies. Worth a read before you sign anything with your vendor.
